Physics-informed spirometry

Incomplete expiratory recordings

A spirometry test uses the amount of air exhaled in the first second (FEV₁) relative to the total forced exhaled volume (FVC) to help identify airway obstruction. If the patient stops blowing too early, FVC can be underestimated and the FEV₁/FVC ratio artificially increased, potentially concealing obstruction. I investigated whether the missing end of the exhalation could be estimated without treating every incomplete test as reliable.

I decoded NHANES flow–volume recordings at 100 samples per second, aligned the start of exhalation by back-extrapolation, and fitted a bounded two-compartment model representing fast and slow lung emptying. Complete recordings provided a reference for testing how accurately the model reconstructed the missing volume.

Reliability-based selection

A fitted curve is not automatically a trustworthy clinical estimate. I therefore compared the physiological reconstruction with ridge regression and built a reliability selector using curve-fit quality, remaining volume change, recording duration and agreement between the two approaches.

At 75% coverage, the selector gives results for the three-quarters of recordings judged most reliable and flags the rest for repetition. I used participant-separated cross-fitting to keep a person’s records from appearing on both sides of model evaluation, then tested the frozen method on separate NHANES survey cycles.

Independent evaluation

On naturally incomplete recordings, reconstruction reduced missed obstruction from 42.7% to 6.7%. At 75% accepted-case coverage in independent NHANES Cycles E and G, sensitivity reached 96.0% and 96.1%, with specificity of 99.7% and 99.8%. Cohen’s kappa was 0.952 and 0.959, indicating high agreement beyond chance.

Compared at the same accepted-case coverage, the physics-informed selector improved kappa over a ridge-feature selector by 0.019 and 0.030 in the two cycles. Paired bootstrap intervals excluded zero, supporting the value of the physiological features for deciding which incomplete recordings could be assessed reliably.
