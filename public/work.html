Human Archive

Business Growth & Machine Learning Data Intern · 18 May–29 June 2026

At Human Archive, a YC-backed robotics-data startup, I developed custom 3D hand-movement models from human demonstrations. The purpose of the representation was to make a manipulation task usable as training data: the observed action needs a consistent description of motion that researchers can relate to a physical task.

I paired recorded actions with 3D hand-movement representations for robot-learning datasets.

I worked on synchronization and quality assurance across visual, depth, motion, and tactile information. Aligning the streams matters because a grasp or contact event has to refer to the same part of the action across modalities. Video supplies the task context, depth supplies geometry, motion describes the hand trajectory, and tactile information records contact; synchronization connects these complementary views to one action example. I also trained and evaluated physical robots on everyday manipulation tasks, using failed grasps and incomplete executions to revise demonstrations and data-quality checks.

The resulting models formed part of data products commercially supplied to six AI laboratories.

I also managed partnerships with 12 manufacturing facilities and helped onboard more than 100 workers. That work determined which real manufacturing actions could be collected and how collection could be coordinated at each facility. It connected the technical representation problem to the operations required to obtain useful demonstrations.

I completed the six-week hybrid internship as the youngest intern in the company’s history.

Wink

On-device retail behaviour analytics

I built Wink’s retail behaviour engine to turn camera detections into measurements of how people move through a store. YOLO detection and tracking provide observations over time; person re-identification helps maintain the relationship between those observations. I then used point-in-polygon tests to assign tracked positions to defined store zones.

A coordinate becomes a visit to a particular area, and tracked entry and exit events supply the timing for zone visits. Aggregating these events connects frame-level detections to dwell time, stopping locations, and movement summaries that a retailer can interpret.

I built the aggregation around questions a retailer can interpret: where shoppers stop, how long they stay, and how movement changes across the store. The dashboard is the reporting layer for these accumulated observations. Its charts expose patterns in footfall and dwell time rather than requiring a retailer to inspect individual video frames.

I moved inference onto retailers’ own computers, reducing the reported monthly operating cost from approximately $500 to $5 while keeping raw footage in the store. This deployment decision shaped the architecture as much as the choice of detector: the useful output is an aggregate record that can be produced locally.

Professor Femida Handy’s academic website

Web developer · University of Pennsylvania, SP2

I designed and implemented Professor Femida Handy’s academic website, organizing her research and professional work for visitors. The structure separates research areas, publications, books, and academic appointments so that a visitor can reach the relevant material without reading a résumé from beginning to end.

I delivered the website within two weeks and incorporated each round of Professor Handy’s feedback.
